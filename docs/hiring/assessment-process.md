# Candidate assessment process

**For:** Hiring managers, recruiters, and interviewers

Use the same process for every candidate. Change only the questions needed to test a risk or claim specific to that person.

The process must answer:

1. Can this person take responsibility for unclear work and bring it to completion?
2. Can this person understand a product problem and make a sensible decision?
3. Can this person work well with the people needed to deliver the result?
4. Can this person learn enough to take on more judgment over time?
5. What is the right entry route if we hire them?

Technical and AI ability is recorded separately. It cannot rescue weak evidence on the main job.

## Stage 1: application review

Read the email, resume, portfolio, and any linked work that matters.

For each important statement, mark it as:

- **Verified:** the source directly shows the fact.
- **Candidate claim:** the candidate says it, but the result or personal contribution is not yet checked.
- **Inference:** a reasonable possibility, but not a fact.
- **Missing:** important evidence that the application does not contain.

Create a short candidate evidence file. Do not score a polished portfolio as proof of ownership before asking what the person did, what failed, and what happened after the first version.

## Stage 2: evidence interview

**Time:** 45 minutes

Tell the candidate:

> We need someone who can take important product work, make it clearer, keep the right people involved, and follow it until the user problem is solved.

Ask for specific past examples. When the candidate says “we”, ask what they personally owned.

### The five main questions

#### 1. Ownership of unclear work

Tell me about a piece of work involving several people that was unclear when it reached you and that you personally brought under control.

Useful probes:

- What did you do yourself?
- How did you track decisions, owners, dependencies, and unfinished work?
- How did you work with someone who was late or disagreed?
- Where did it get stuck, and how did it end?

#### 2. Completion after the first version

Tell me about the last thing you followed through to a real result.

Useful probes:

- What happened after the first version was ready?
- What did you test or check yourself?
- What remained incomplete, and who experienced the result?

#### 3. A difficult priority choice

Tell me about a time when your team had more important work than it could finish.

Useful probes:

- What did you delay or reject?
- Who disagreed, and what evidence mattered?
- What would have changed your decision?

#### 4. Product judgment

Tell me about a request that first looked like a product change but needed a different answer.

Useful probes:

- How did you find the underlying problem?
- What did you recommend and choose not to build?
- How did you learn whether the answer worked?

#### 5. Learning after feedback

Tell me about feedback that changed how you work.

Useful probes:

- What were you doing before?
- What changed in your next piece of work?
- What shows that the change lasted?

### How to use candidate-specific questions

Choose no more than three before the interview. Use them to test the largest claims or risks in the candidate's evidence file.

Do not add a second full interview. Replace a main question or use the final ten minutes.

### When to stop

Stop the process if the interview gives clear evidence that the candidate cannot meet the required ownership, organisation, communication, judgment, or teamwork level.

Do not give a work sample merely to complete the process.

## Stage 3: standard work sample

**Time:** 90 minutes, followed by a 20 minute discussion

Give every continuing candidate the same booking-to-tenant exercise and the same instructions.

The candidate may use notes, the internet, and AI tools. Score the decision and the plan, not the appearance of the document.

A strong answer should usually notice that:

- automatic conversion helps many properties
- different properties may need different behaviour
- approval and actual move-in are not always the same event
- a manual process can cause real move-ins to be forgotten
- welcome messages should follow activation, not only a date
- changing behaviour can create unclear old cases
- the first release should stay focused
- Design, Engineering, QA, Customer Success, and Product need clear next steps
- the answer must include testing, release, and follow-up

The candidate may recommend a different solution. Judge the reasoning, trade-offs, and completeness of the plan.

### Work sample discussion

Ask the candidate to explain the answer in five minutes.

Then ask:

1. Which part are you least confident about?
2. What would you check first with access to the product and team?
3. What did you choose not to solve now?
4. What would change if most properties strongly value automatic conversion?
5. What would you do if managers began missing manual move-ins?

Look for whether new evidence changes the answer rather than only making it longer.

## Stage 4: technical and AI exercise

Use this only after the candidate meets the main job level.

Give the fictional booking data exercise. Allow any suitable tool. Ask for a usable action list, the method, the uncertain records, and instructions another person can follow tomorrow.

A strong result:

- identifies records that need action
- separates urgent work from later follow-up
- catches missing, invalid, duplicate, or conflicting data
- keeps uncertain records out of unsafe actions
- produces something another person can reuse
- explains assumptions and limits plainly

Do not reward complexity. A correct spreadsheet can be better than an unfinished application.

### Assessor check for the data exercise

- `B001`, `B004`, and `B014` need prompt move-in or conversion action.
- `B013` is overdue, but the partial payment needs a decision that the data cannot safely make.
- `B007` has no joining date. `B009` has an invalid date.
- `B010` appears twice with conflicting status and needs reconciliation.
- `B011` is active but has no welcome message recorded.
- `B002` reaches its joining date today. The timing assumption should be stated.
- `B005` needs an approval decision, not a move-in.
- `B003` and `B008` need no move-in action.
- `B006` and `B012` are future bookings and may need later follow-up.

## Stage 5: references and decision

Use the [candidate scorecard](candidate-scorecard.md). Each assessor records evidence and scores before discussing the candidate.

References should test the main risk found during the assessment. Ask questions such as:

1. What work did this person own without repeated reminders?
2. Tell me about a time they failed to finish or missed an important detail. What happened next?
3. How did they work when another team was slow or disagreed?
4. What kind of work would you not yet trust them to own alone?
5. What should their next manager do in the first month to get the best work from them?

Ask for examples. A general positive reference should not erase a specific concern.

## Decide fit before entry route

First decide whether the candidate fits the APM job.

Then decide the route:

- Recommend the internship intended to convert when the candidate has strong potential but needs to prove consistent work in a real team and product setting.
- Recommend direct full-time with normal probation when the candidate has repeated professional evidence of doing this job at the required level.

Probation does not replace assessment. Internship should not be used to underlevel an experienced candidate.

## Finish every assessment

Use the seven-line decision note in the candidate scorecard. Record the evidence, main risk, missing fact, technical signal, entry route, and one next action.
