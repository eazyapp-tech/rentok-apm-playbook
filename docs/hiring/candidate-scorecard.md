# Candidate scorecard

**For:** Interviewers and hiring decision makers

Keep one scorecard per assessor for every candidate.

Record direct evidence and scores before discussing the candidate with another assessor. After the live discussion, add the new evidence and record any score change with its reason. Do not erase the earlier score. Use Not observed when a stage did not produce enough evidence.

## Score meanings

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Not observed | The assessment did not produce enough evidence. |
| 1 | Clear evidence below the level needed for this role. |
| 2 | Can do parts of the work but would need frequent direction or reminders. |
| 3 | Ready for this role with normal support and room to learn. |
| 4 | Unusually strong evidence. The person improves the quality or pace of the work around them. |

## Required areas

| Area | What we are judging | Score | Observed evidence | Risk or missing evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ownership and follow-through | Takes responsibility, moves blocked work, and stays involved through release and follow-up. | | | |
| Product judgment | Finds the real problem, weighs conflicting evidence, and makes sensible trade-offs. | | | |
| Execution and organisation | Turns a decision into a clear journey, next steps, responsibilities, testing, rollout, and follow-up. | | | |
| Communication and collaboration | Writes and speaks plainly and works constructively with Product, Design, Engineering, Quality Assurance, and Customer Success. | | | |

All five areas are required. Do not calculate an average.

Ownership and follow-through usually need evidence from past work or references. A take-home exercise can support this score but cannot prove long-term follow-through by itself.

## Using AI in Product work

Score the evidence, but do not use a fixed hiring or entry-route threshold.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Not observed | The assessment did not produce enough evidence. |
| 1 | Uses AI carelessly, resists learning, or cannot take responsibility for the result. |
| 2 | Has basic or limited experience but shows curiosity, learns through real tasks, and accepts guidance. |
| 3 | Uses AI thoughtfully, checks important work, and remains accountable for the result. |
| 4 | Builds repeatable ways of working that improve their own work or the team's work. |

## Decision rule

### Strong yes

- Every required area scores at least 3.
- At least one required area has clear evidence for a 4.
- The application, recruiter screen, work sample, live discussion, and references available at that point tell a consistent story.
- There is no serious unresolved concern.

### Yes

- Every required area scores at least 3.
- The candidate can do the role with normal support.
- Any development areas are manageable and clearly recorded.

### Hold

- One specific missing fact could change the decision.
- Record the fact, who will check it, and by when.
- General uncertainty is not enough.

### No

- A required area remains below 3 after the relevant assessment.
- The candidate cannot explain or take responsibility for important parts of the submitted work.
- There is serious evidence of dishonesty, unreliability, unsafe behaviour, or refusal to learn.

Do not change a required score because the candidate has a strong Uxcel result, uses AI well, has technical ability, or presents confidently.

## Entry route

Choose the entry route after deciding that the candidate meets the job bar.

- **Internship intended to convert:** The candidate meets the bar but has limited repeated evidence from real professional work.
- **Direct full-time:** The candidate meets the bar and already has repeated professional evidence of doing comparable work. This normally includes RentOk's probation terms.

Probation does not replace assessment. Do not use an internship to underlevel an experienced candidate.

AI experience and Uxcel results do not decide the entry route. Any exception to normal probation should follow company policy and have a clear reason.
