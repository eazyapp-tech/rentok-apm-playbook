# APM KRAs and success review

**For:** APMs and the Product Lead

These are the areas used to review the APM's work.

<table>
<tr>
<th>Area</th>
<th>What good work looks like</th>
<th>Evidence we should be able to see</th>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>1. Ownership and completion</td>
<td>Work moves from a problem to a checked result without repeated reminders from the Product Lead.</td>
<td>Clear owners and next steps, early action on blockers, product acceptance, release follow-up.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>2. Organisation and clarity</td>
<td>Scattered information becomes a clear problem, decision, scope, risk, and plan.</td>
<td>Current notes, clear status, written decisions, useful updates, fewer repeated questions.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>3. Product judgment</td>
<td>The APM understands the real user problem and recommends a sensible answer without adding unnecessary work.</td>
<td>Evidence behind choices, stated assumptions, rejected options, clear trade-offs, learning after release.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>4. Working with other teams</td>
<td>Design, Engineering, QA, and customer-facing teams have the context they need and can raise concerns early.</td>
<td>Fewer avoidable misunderstandings, useful questions, clear context when work moves between teams, constructive disagreement.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>5. Quality and user result</td>
<td>The APM stays responsible through testing, release, and user follow-up.</td>
<td>Realistic UAT, known limits, release readiness, support review, adoption or outcome checks.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>6. Learning and independence</td>
<td>The same kind of problem needs less direction over time.</td>
<td>Feedback changes behaviour, context is learned, safe decisions are made independently, risks are raised with a recommendation.</td>
</tr>
</table>

## How to review performance

Use examples from actual work. Do not judge from how busy the person looked or how many documents they produced.

Ask:

- What did this person own?
- What became clearer or moved faster because of them?
- What needed repeated reminders?
- What decision did they make, and what evidence supported it?
- What happened after release?
- What can they now handle with less help?

For an intern being considered for conversion, use the same evidence. Completing the internship period by itself is not enough.

## Technical and AI ability

Record this separately.

It is useful when the person can use code, data, APIs, prototypes, automation, or AI tools to investigate a problem or complete useful work with less help.

It does not make up for weak ownership, poor communication, or careless product decisions.

## What not to reward by itself

- number of PRDs written
- number of Jira tickets updated
- number of meetings attended
- number of features released
- technical complexity
- activity without a clear result
