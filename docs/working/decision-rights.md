# Decision rights and escalation

**For:** APMs, the Product Lead, and teams working with them

The aim is to let the APM make more decisions as their context and judgment grow without creating avoidable risk.

The employment route does not decide these rights. An experienced hire may earn wider decision rights sooner. Every new joiner should first learn the relevant product and business rules.

## Usually decide yourself

- how to collect missing evidence
- how to classify and group issues
- routine product questions where expected behaviour is already clear
- how to organise active product information
- small and reversible choices that do not change the main product rules
- product test notes for work you own

Write down decisions that other people need to understand later.

## Recommend, then align before finalising

- priority between important pieces of work
- scope changes that alter the user result
- important workflow changes
- decisions that affect several product areas
- changes to permissions, money, or important operating rules
- public customer promises or dates

Bring a recommendation and explain the trade-off.

## Ask for help early

- money, customer trust, compliance, privacy, or data integrity may be at risk
- a decision may affect many customers
- important priorities conflict
- the decision changes a main RentOk product rule
- a critical dependency cannot be resolved
- you strongly disagree with the proposed direction
- you do not have enough evidence to make a responsible decision

Asking for help is not failure. Asking without first doing the available thinking is not enough.

A useful escalation says:

> This could change payment reconciliation across several properties. I found two possible answers. I recommend B because it keeps the current ledger rule, but it changes the refund journey. I need a decision on whether we accept that change.

## Product and Engineering responsibilities

**Product and the APM own:**

- user and business problem
- priority and product scope
- product behaviour and business rules
- product questions and acceptance
- Product UAT and follow-up after release

**Engineering owns:**

- architecture and implementation
- technical estimates and capacity
- technical quality and dependencies
- engineering work and people management

Both sides should raise uncertainty early and agree on the result the release must achieve.
