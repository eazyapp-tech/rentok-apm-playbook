# Starting the APM role

**For:** New APMs and their Product Lead

> **Your job at RentOk:** Help us turn important problems into useful products. Make the work clear and stay responsible until we know what happened for the user.

This guide applies whether you join through an internship or as a full-time employee.

An experienced hire may move through the first steps faster. Nobody should skip learning RentOk's users, product, and way of working.

## Why this role exists

RentOk has many products, users, and pieces of work moving at the same time.

Important work can fail in small ways. The problem was not understood. A decision stayed in a message. Design and Engineering used different assumptions. A blocker had no owner. Testing covered the happy path only. The release went live, but nobody checked whether users understood it.

Your first responsibility is to make the work you own dependable.

Your next responsibility is to build enough judgment to make more product decisions yourself.

## What ownership means here

When you own a piece of work, you should be able to answer:

- What problem are we solving?
- Who has this problem?
- What evidence do we have?
- What have we decided?
- What is still uncertain?
- Where is the latest design or product note?
- Who is doing the next part?
- What is blocked or at risk?
- What must be tested?
- Is it ready to release?
- What happened after release?

These answers should not live only in your memory.

Ownership does not mean doing every person's job. It means noticing when work is unclear or stuck, finding the right person, and helping the work move.

## How to handle an unclear assignment

Do not wait for someone to write the whole answer for you.

First, investigate. Speak to the right people. Check the current product, earlier decisions, user examples, data, tickets, and technical limits that matter.

Then return with:

1. **What I understood:** the problem, user, and desired result.
2. **What I found:** the facts that matter and where they came from.
3. **What I recommend:** the decision you think we should make and why.
4. **What I would leave out:** work that does not belong in the current answer.
5. **What still needs a decision:** only the questions that could change the recommendation.
6. **What happens next:** the owner and next step for each important part.

As your judgment grows, the list in section 5 should become smaller.

## Your main responsibilities

### Understand and prepare work

- Find the real problem before writing a solution.
- Collect only the context needed to make and carry out the decision.
- Make rules, states, permissions, edge cases, and acceptance checks clear.
- Keep the current decision and current source easy to find.

### Work with Design

Give Design the user and business context they need.

Review whether the design solves the problem, covers the complete journey, handles important states, and uses language a real RentOk user will understand.

### Work with Engineering

Product owns the user and business problem, priority, product behaviour, scope, business rules, product questions, and product acceptance.

Engineering owns architecture, implementation, technical estimates, technical quality, technical risks, and engineering management.

Work closely enough that uncertainty becomes visible before it turns into delay or rework.

### Work with QA

QA does not remove Product's responsibility for quality.

Use the product yourself. Test realistic journeys. Check important limits and failure states. Make sure the result matches the decision, not only the ticket.

### Release and follow-up

Before release, confirm product acceptance, critical defects, known limits, internal preparation, and any communication users need.

After release, check usage, support questions, customer feedback, new defects, and whether the original problem improved.

### Stay close to customers

Spend time with customers, Customer Success, Sales, support cases, and the actual product.

RentOk users run work in real buildings with staff, tenants, money, and time pressure. Understand that work before deciding how the software should behave.

## How to write

Write so that a smart reader can understand the meaning, decision, and required action without you sitting beside them.

Use plain words. Explain necessary technical terms once. Keep one idea in one sentence when possible.

Write the smallest useful document. A short decision note can be better than a long PRD. Important behaviour should still be written down.

## How to use AI

Use available AI tools when they make the work clearer, better checked, or more systematic. Useful applications may include understanding a problem, exploring options, improving a document, questioning assumptions, keeping context organised, or reducing repeated work.

AI is a tool, not the owner of the work. If you submit, share, recommend, or act on an output, you are responsible for checking it and for what happens next.

You cannot explain a mistake by saying that AI produced the work and you forgot to check it.

Match the check to the possible harm:

- Read and edit early thinking or a rough draft.
- Check important claims, sources, assumptions, and calculations in research or analysis.
- Check accuracy, tone, context, and consequences before sending customer communication or publishing work.
- Independently verify work involving money, access, personal information, legal commitments, or production changes. Get the required approval before acting.

Use approved tools and accounts for company work. Do not place customer, employee, financial, credential, or other sensitive information into an unapproved AI tool. Remove identifying information when full detail is not needed.

You do not need to know every tool. You are expected to learn through real work, respond to feedback, and improve how you use AI over time.

## How independence should grow

At first, you may ask whether your understanding is correct.

Next, you should bring options and a recommendation.

Then, you should make safe and reversible decisions, explain the risk, and ask for help only where it matters.

Eventually, you should own a product area and keep the Product Lead informed without needing them in every conversation.

Your work should make the people around you less confused. After you become involved, the problem, decision, risk, owner, and next step should be easier to understand.
