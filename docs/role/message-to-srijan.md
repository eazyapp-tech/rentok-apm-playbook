# What we are hiring for

**Internal message to Srijan**

Srijan,

I am clearer now about the Product person I want us to hire.

The immediate need is someone who can take ownership of active Product work and follow it properly. Today, many small but important things come back to me. A reported issue has to be understood. A feature has to be made ready. Design and Engineering need answers. Blockers need owners. QA and Product have to test the work. A release has to be followed after it goes live.

I need an APM who becomes the person who knows what is happening around the work they own. They should know the problem, current decision, latest design, Engineering status, QA status, risk, and next step. I should not have to remind them about every unfinished part.

I do not want someone who only updates Jira or schedules meetings. They need real Product potential. If I give them a vague problem, they should investigate it, understand the user, consider the options, and come back with a recommendation. As they learn, I should need to give them less direction.

The main things I would look for are:

- reliable ownership and follow-through
- the ability to bring order to messy work
- clear and simple communication
- good judgment about the real problem
- comfort working with Design, Engineering, QA, and customer-facing teams
- willingness to test, follow releases, and check what happened for users
- fast learning and openness to feedback
- willingness to do small or repetitive work properly

Technical ability and using AI in Product work are two separate things.

Coding is not required. Technical comfort is useful. This may include understanding APIs, using basic SQL, reading a simple implementation, making a prototype, or automating a task. It is an additional advantage, not the main requirement.

Using AI well in everyday Product work is also important. It can make thinking, writing, checking, organisation, learning, and follow-through easier and more systematic. I do not expect every good candidate to have mature AI workflows already. Many candidates have not had the same exposure or opportunity.

We should not reject someone with strong conventional Product experience only because their AI experience is limited. We should look for curiosity, the ability to learn through a real task, careful checking, and willingness to improve how they work.

AI is only a tool. The person remains responsible for the work. They cannot explain a mistake by saying that AI produced it and they forgot to check. Strong AI ability also cannot make up for weak ownership or poor Product judgment.

I see two valid ways to hire for this role.

For an early-career candidate, we can offer a paid internship that is genuinely intended to convert to full-time when the work is strong. Conversion is based on observed work, not only on completing the internship period.

If we find someone who already has enough relevant experience and evidence, we should be comfortable making a direct full-time offer even if their AI workflow is still developing. This would normally include our probation terms. Any exception should follow company policy and have a clear reason.

We should not make a strong experienced person take an internship only because that is how we first described the opening. We should also not use probation instead of doing a proper assessment.

In both routes, the job is the same. The difference is how much evidence the person already has and how quickly we expect them to work independently.

The long-term goal is to develop another strong Product Manager who can own meaningful parts of RentOk without everything passing through me.
